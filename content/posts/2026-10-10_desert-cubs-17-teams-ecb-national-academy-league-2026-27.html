Chapter 02

From 11 Young Cricketers to 17 Competitive Teams

The Desert Cubs story began in October 2007.

At a corner practice net of Sharjah International Cricket Stadium, founder Presley Polonnowita started a journey with just 11 young cricketers and a vision to nurture the next generation of sporting talent.

There were no 17-team contingents, extensive training-centre networks or thousands of alumni.

There was simply a belief that young cricketers deserved quality coaching, strong values and opportunities to test themselves in competitive cricket.

Nineteen years later, that belief has grown into one of the UAE's most established junior cricket development programmes.

Today, Desert Cubs operates five training centres across Dubai and Sharjah, has trained more than 15,000 young players over its history, and continues to provide structured cricket development from the earliest stages of the game through competitive youth cricket.

The academy's journey has included national league achievements, international cricket tours, specialist coaching, major events and opportunities for young players to learn from established international cricketers.

The 2026/27 ECB National Academy League contingent is another chapter in that story.

What began with 11 players has grown into an academy capable of bringing together more than 220 young cricketers for a single national league season.

This milestone was not built overnight. It has been 19 years in the making.

Chapter 04

A Proud Legacy in National Cricket

This historic entry builds on years of Desert Cubs participation and achievements in national youth cricket.

The academy's competitive history includes the ECB Women's League championship in 2017, success in the ECB Under 17 National Academy League in 2017/18, the Under 14 National Academy League title in 2018/19, the Under 19 National Academy League title in 2019/20, and Under 16 National League successes in subsequent seasons.

These achievements reflect a long-term commitment to player development and competitive exposure.

Desert Cubs has also provided international opportunities through cricket tours to destinations including England, Australia, Sri Lanka and South Africa, helping young players experience new conditions, opponents and cricketing environments.

Those experiences matter.

They teach young cricketers to adapt, work with their teammates and manage the pressure of competition.

The ECB National Academy League is another important part of that development journey.

Every training session prepares players for a challenge.

Every competitive match offers an opportunity to learn.

And every season provides another chance to improve.

Chapter 06

Cricket Development Is More Than Bat and Ball

At Desert Cubs, we believe that developing a successful cricketer requires more than natural talent.

Technical skills matter, but so do discipline, physical fitness, teamwork, mental strength, confidence and the ability to perform under pressure.

Our coaching environment is designed to help players build these qualities progressively.

Training takes place across our five centres:

Sharjah English School (SES) — Sharjah

Star International School (SIS) — Al Qusais, Dubai

Deira International School (DIS) — Dubai Festival City

Delhi Private School (DPS) — Jebel Ali, Dubai

Baseline Sports Academy (BSA) — Dubai Investment Park

Together, these centres support a pathway from foundational cricket to structured competition.

Through regular coaching, match practice, fitness sessions, internal tournaments and external competitive opportunities, Desert Cubs aims to help young players develop both their cricketing ability and their character.

Participation across 17 national academy league teams demonstrates the scale of that pathway.

But our goal has never been simply to produce a greater number of teams.

Our goal is to develop better-prepared young cricketers.

Chapter 07

Our Girls Are Part of the Journey

The Desert Cubs story would not be complete without recognising the contribution of girls' and women's cricket.

Our girls' cricket programme has been part of the academy's development journey for many years.

In 2017, Desert Cubs celebrated a landmark achievement by becoming champions of the inaugural ECB Women's League.

The academy's alumni also include Esha Oza, who has gone on to represent and captain the UAE Women's National Cricket Team.

These achievements demonstrate what sustained encouragement, coaching and competitive opportunities can contribute to the development of women's cricket.

The inclusion of a girls' team in our 2026/27 national academy league contingent is another important part of that continuing commitment.

Because the future of UAE cricket belongs to both boys and girls.

And every young cricketer deserves the opportunity to pursue their ambitions.
