Desert Cubs UK Tour 2023 — Official Highlights

The Journey Begins

As we look forward with excitement to the 2026 UK Tour, we take a heartfelt moment to look back at the incredible journey our young cricketers completed in the summer of 2023. From the 14th to the 26th of July 2023, Desert Cubs squads — the Cheetahs, Panthers, Jaguars, and Leopards — embarked on an unforgettable adventure to Worksop, Nottingham, for the prestigious ICCA Global Academy Championship.

It was a tour defined by resilience, excellence, and the creation of memories that these young cricketers will carry with them for the rest of their lives. Competing against top-tier academies from the USA, UK, India, and Sri Lanka, our cubs showed exactly what Desert Cubs is made of.

Training at the Home of Cricket — Lord's

The undisputed highlight for every player and parent on the tour was the once-in-a-lifetime visit to Lord's Cricket Ground — the undisputed Home of Cricket. Our young cubs didn't simply tour the museum. They experienced an intensive one-hour training session in the legendary Lord's indoor stadium.

Standing where legends have stood — Tendulkar, Lara, Flintoff — and training in that hallowed ground was an inspiration that will drive these players for years to come. The look on their faces when they stepped onto that turf is something no parent, coach, or manager will ever forget.

Championship Glory

Despite the unpredictable English weather, our teams showed incredible adaptability and the true champion's spirit. The ICCA Global Academy Championship at Worksop featured some of the best junior cricket academies from across the globe — and Desert Cubs rose to the challenge.

More Than Just Trophies

The 2023 tour wasn't just about the silverware — though we are immensely proud of every trophy and award our cubs brought home. It was about the growth of our players as individuals, the relationships forged in hotel corridors and on training grounds, and the quiet moments of realisation that happened when a young cricketer from Dubai stood in the outfield at a Nottinghamshire ground and understood, perhaps for the first time, what it truly means to love this game.

It was about the nights where coaches and managers sat with players, talking through match plans, reviewing their own performances with the kind of self-awareness that no classroom can teach. It was about adapting — to the swing, to the seam, to the grey skies and the cold mornings that are so foreign to our Dubai-raised cubs.
